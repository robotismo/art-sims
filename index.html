Generative art · four volumes

Art Simulations

Seventeen generative simulations that run live in the browser. The Leaf Atlas grows leaf structure from first principles; Cosmos pushes millions of particles through velocity fields on the GPU; Emergence draws order out of a few local rules; Magnum Opus gathers the series into one tapestry of light. Open any tile to run it.

Pieces are numbered in the order they were made, so 14–16 sit in Vol. I, which they joined later.

Art Simulations · Vol. I

The Leaf Atlas

Eight generative simulations that grow leaf structure from first principles — an algorithmic answer to the lamm-mit leaf-inspired image generation collection, where MIT's Markus Buehler lab fine-tunes diffusion models on <leaf microstructure> to design bio-materials. Where they sample, these compute.

Art Simulations · Vol. II

Cosmos

Beyond the leaf. The first GPU-driven pieces — millions of particles advected on the graphics card, where the same handful of velocity fields that move smoke, chaos, and galaxies are made visible at once.

Art Simulations · Vol. III

Emergence

Order without a designer — three studies where elaborate structure falls out of a few local rules: a lightning fractal, a fluid you can stir, and a flock with a hawk.

Art Simulations · Vol. IV

Magnum Opus

One piece that gathers what the series learned — divergence-free flow, velocity-streak rendering, an evolving timeline — into a tapestry of millions of light-filaments that passes through four movements and never repeats.

How they are built. Every piece is a single self-contained HTML file with no build step — open one and read its source. The Leaf Atlas and Emergence draw on the 2D canvas in plain JavaScript. Cosmos and Magnum Opus run on three.js r184 with WebGL2 GPGPU (positions and velocities live in floating-point textures), served from this site rather than a CDN.
